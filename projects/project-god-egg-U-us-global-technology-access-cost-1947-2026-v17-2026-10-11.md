## Historical boundary and attribution
- 1947-12-16: Bardeen and Brattain obtain first semiconductor point-contact transistor amplification at Bell Labs; demonstrated 1947-12-23; announced June 1948. Invention forms a **semiconductor technology accounting anchor**, not the date of the Internet.
  Source: https://www.computerhistory.org/siliconengine/invention-of-the-point-contact-transistor/
- 1969: ARPANET first host and first host-to-host message; four nodes by the end of year.
- 1983-01-01: ARPANET transition to TCP/IP.
  Source: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet/
- 1991 onward: web access/distribution and subsequent commercial internet platforms, later mobile/cloud/AI. Milestones form a conceptual historical sequence, not a single measured 79-year internet expenditure account.
- Boundary 1947 to 2026 spans **79 calendar-year difference**; 2026 data below represent latest explicitly dated international estimates, often 2025, not a fabricated 2026 population count.

## Measured global access baseline
ITU *Facts and Figures 2025*, released 2025-11-17:
- **6.0 billion online, 2.2 billion offline** worldwide, 2025.
- **94% online in high-income countries vs 23% in low-income countries**, a **71-percentage-point gap**.
- 96% of people offline in low- and middle-income countries; 77% men online vs 71% women; 85% urban vs 58% rural.
Sources:
https://www.itu.int/en/mediacentre/Pages/PR-2025-11-17-Facts-and-Figures.aspx
https://www.itu.int/itu-d/reports/statistics/facts-figures-2025/

## Global closing-the-gap funding benchmark
ITU / CST *Connecting Humanity Action Blueprint*, released **2025-09-01**: **USD 2.6–2.8 trillion** estimated investment to achieve **universal meaningful connectivity by 2030**, at the study's 2025 current prices. This is a prospective funding requirement including quality, affordability and skills, NOT historical total internet production cost, not a book's revenue, not legal restitution and not funds held in a common treasury.

Breakdown reported by ITU:
- Hard digital infrastructure: **USD 1.5–1.7T**.
- Devices and broadband affordability: **USD 983B**.
- Digital literacy / skills: **USD 152B**.
- Policy and regulation: **USD 600M**.
Independent high/low item sums are ~$2.636–2.836T, differing slightly from headline $2.6–2.8T due to published rounding. Do not force item totals to match artificial precision.
Source: https://www.itu.int/en/mediacentre/Pages/PR-2025-09-01-Connecting-Humanity.aspx

ITU 2025 affordability: a data-only mobile broadband price basket globally median 1.4% of per-capita GNI, fixed 2.5%; low-income country baskets are far more onerous, with entry-level 2%-of-GNI target unmet in many settings. GNI per capita is a **comparison denominator** and not household disposable income. A basket's affordability should not be assumed equal for rich and poor households within one economy.
Sources:
https://www.itu.int/itu-d/reports/statistics/2025/10/15/ff25-affordability-of-ict-services/
https://www.itu.int/itu-d/reports/statistics/2025/11/17/gcr-2025-chapter-4/

## Other progress costs (not additive to ITU funding ask)
- IEA *Energy and AI* 2025: global data-centre electricity consumption ~**415 TWh in 2024**, about 1.5% of global electricity demand. This includes AI and other data-centre services; it is NOT all Internet electricity. The IEA 2025 Base Case projected ~945 TWh by 2030, scenario not observed reality.
  Source: https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/energy-demand-from-ai
- ITU/UNITAR *Global E-waste Monitor 2024*: **62 million tonnes** worldwide electronic waste in 2022; only **22.3%** formally documented collection/recycling. Entire electronics waste stream, NOT solely internet hardware. WHO associates unsafe handling with environmental and human-health risks.
  Sources: https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-D/Environment/Pages/Publications/The-Global-E-Waste-Monitor-2024.aspx
           https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/children-and-digital-dumpsites-e-waste-and-health

## Fairness and responsibility
The internet connects social, governmental and corporate resources. Lack of access may reflect infrastructure, prices, disability access, power supply, skills, regulation or content suitability. The presence of a barrier does NOT prove that every entity intentionally excluded users nor that a specific monetary debt exists. The ledger must identify actors, obligations, local beneficiaries, consent, environmental responsibility, conditions for effective access, and a source. A universal *opportunity to participate* is a proposed design objective, not a license to seize private assets or force usage.

The book *1931 TO NOW* discusses U.S. historical wealth inequality and algorithmic inheritance; the 1947/global digital access extension is NEW external research and should not be attributed to the book as if included in it.
