The user meant **civilization-scale, turn-based strategic competition**, **not** the standard 52-card game called War. The preceding million-game card-War experiment (v4.9) therefore answers the **wrong question**; retain it as an archived experiment, not evidence for the intended civilization-style game.

## Minimum user-given rules, preserved
1. There is **one judge and two players**.
2. The judge initially permits a player to choose the game.
3. The players play the chosen game.
4. **The winner chooses the next move / next game and its rules** (the user's phrasing across turns).
5. **The game you win is the life you live.**
6. Each successive game introduces **one new element**; the user refers to the combinatorial growth using `n!` and a potential `24!` search.
7. **The judge judges**—observes, evaluates the agreed conditions, and reports results, without competing.
8. Do not automatically or arbitrarily force the winner to occupy the judge role, pre-specify winners, or impose a 24-game recurrence.

Potential state dimensions, **not yet user-approved rules**, include territory, population, material resources, technology, production, diplomacy, living conditions, ecological viability, and time/money/health costs. "War" may be combat or a broader strategic contest among civilizations; choosing peace, diplomacy, nonaggression or observer-role changes are potential meaningful decisions if the rules allow them.

**Crucially:** victory in a destructive contest may leave the winner with a damaged world to inhabit. This is a philosophical consequence of the user's winning-game/lived-life premise, not an automatic mathematical win/loss theorem.

## What remains undefined
The user's minimalist rules do not set turn budgets, geography, civilization starting conditions, combat mechanics, victory conditions, actor preferences, or which rules persist when winners select new games. These must be supplied by the players (or agreed before a simulation), **not secretly invented by the judge**. A million **turns** and a million **complete matches** are different simulation requests.
