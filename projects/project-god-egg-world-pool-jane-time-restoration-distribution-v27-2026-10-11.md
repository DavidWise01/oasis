## What counts as the World Pool

Track source, operator/steward, rights and operational availability for each resource. The ledger admits ONLY legitimately shareable, licensed, contributed, public, or otherwise authorized capacity, not a blanket confiscation of global infrastructure.

```text
RESOURCE_CLASSES:
  - knowledge / public education / licensed content
  - internet connectivity and bandwidth
  - devices and accessible interfaces
  - compute, storage and software services
  - electricity and operational resilience
  - technical training, support and maintenance
  - legally available shared facilities and research
```

**Nonrival** knowledge can often be copied subject to copyright/consent; **rival** bandwidth, device stock, GPU-hours, electricity and staff time need capacity accounting and replenishment. Never assume a fixed money amount represents the complete pool.

## Distribution: ordered lexicographic rule

**Rule 0 — recognition.** Register each qualifying {{i}} as a 100% member with 1:1 standing. Require identity minimally sufficient to prevent duplication and fraud; avoid unnecessary personally identifying data.

**Rule 1 — universal floor.** Target meaningful minimum access for every {{i}} according to ability and legitimate need. For an under-served participant, provisioning a missing device, accessibility accommodation, training or connection may come before allocating excess scarce compute to a participant above the floor.

**Rule 2 — repair unequal access.** Among those below the floor, prioritize the biggest measured access deficits and barriers (distance, price, lack of skills, disability-related accessibility, network coverage, etc.), not historic wealth or prestige.

**Rule 3 — time restoration.** With the floor adequately safeguarded, use audited net usable time restored per constrained resource, with protections against concentration. Avoid maximizing saved-hours alone in ways that systematically exclude people whose time recovery is difficult to quantify.

**Rule 4 — renewal and safety.** Retain enough capacity for maintenance, sustainability, disaster recovery and accessibility. Staff time, environmental impacts, consent and licensing remain constraints, not invisible externalities. Allow independent appeals and public reporting of aggregate effects.

This is **priority order**, not percentages of users or a 12/9/6/3 split. The earlier 12/9/6/3 monetary scenario, if revisited, is a separate authorized funding tool; it never modifies the pool's 1:1 membership.

## Time-restoration measure

```text
task = a meaningful goal selected by participating {{i}}
T_before = total relevant effort/wait/travel needed to complete task before aid
T_after  = total relevant effort/wait/travel + training + upkeep after aid
T_restored = max(0, T_before - T_after)
```

Preserve negative effects separately as `T_added`; `max(0,...)` must never erase harm. For unrealized opportunities, use a **separate opportunity-delay metric** with explicit counterfactual assumptions, rather than creating fictional hours. Compute cycles, human hours, calendar delay and staffing effort are *different units*; do not add them without an explicit, agreed utility mapping. If savings merely shift uncompensated work to someone else, log the other participant's loss and net the relevant impacts across the full scope.
