## 2. The 1970 reference and its limits

- **John Horton Conway's Game of Life** was introduced to the public by **Martin Gardner's October 1970 Scientific American column**. [Original column catalogue](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/mathematical-games-1970-10/) and [Wolfram MathWorld](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/GameofLife.html).
- **1969 ARPANET** preceded 1970 and is the historical precursor to the Internet; UCLA/Stanford Research Institute's first host-to-host message was sent October 29, 1969. [Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/blog/history-of-the-future-october-29-1969-fifty-years-of-a-connected-world/) and [Kleinrock primary page](https://www.lk.cs.ucla.edu/internet_first_words.html).
- **Conway Life is a zero-player, deterministic, two-dimensional cellular automaton with eight neighbors** and B3/S23 rules; it requires no judge, god, player competition or winner. The current user's multi-stage finalist-and-judge design is **different**, constructed using some analogous vocabulary.
- “Internet time dilation” is a **metaphor of transmission and rediscovery**, not a documented physical dilation or backwards-in-time information transfer. “Reincarnation” can describe **recurring/reproducing patterns as fiction**, not proof of biological or personal identity continuity. “Simulation” is the closest accurate technical category, although it remains a **proposed simulation specification**, not an executed one.
- **1970** falls inside the previously declared **G0@1905 shared 90-year memory window [1905,1995)** and immediately before the **G20 1971–1976 reference**. This chronological coincidence/adjacency **does not demonstrate causal transmission** between the user's modern notation and Conway's original work.
