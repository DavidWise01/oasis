II. Divergence (1945–1968): GI Bill, suburban segregation, Brown/Montgomery, Equal Pay and Civil Rights Acts, Kerner Commission.
III. Stall (1968–1990): affirmative action, ERA, Reaganomics, sentencing/incarceration.
IV. Acceleration (1990–2008): NAFTA/welfare reform, mass incarceration, subprime, Great Recession.
V. Present (2010–2026): unequal asset recovery, gig work, COVID, algorithmic inequality, contemporary gap snapshot.
VI. Arithmetic: compounding example and closing-gap scenarios.
Appendices: wage data, wealth data, policy chronology, sources/methodology, glossary.

## Book-derived anchors, NOT additive
- Ch 1: estimated 1931–64 construction wage loss $25–30 billion (book describes 2024-dollar federal construction basis); modeled compounded impact $200–500 billion by 2026. The latter is an accumulation *of* historical loss, not a separate debt to sum.
- Ch 6: book estimates $4–6 trillion white-family wealth creation via GI Bill, a comparison / generated-benefit estimate, not automatically recoverable unpaid debt.
- Ch 16: book cites $1.2 trillion/year economy-wide incarceration cost, not a transfer payable to a specified group.
- Ch 22: book compares 2022 median white household wealth $285,000 and Black median household wealth $44,900; median ratio ~6.3:1. Medians are not additive household balances.
- Ch 23: book's illustrative 95-year 5% compounding with 1.00 vs 0.60 income gives nominal savings outcomes ~$204.07 vs ~$122.44 and 1.67:1 ratio. It is a scenario using fixed normalized annual contributions; not a reconstruction of actual households. Book interprets difference versus 6.3:1 observed median ratio; its “27%/73%” decomposition should not be treated as a validated attribution without further methods.
- Ch 24: author-calculated $14.5 trillion aggregate racial wealth-parity gap (2022): **benchmark**, not judicial finding or automatically due amount.
- Ch 24: proposal discussed via Darity/Mullen $10–14 trillion direct-payment reparations; potential policy remedy, not an obligation already established.
- Ch 24: baby bonds $60–80 billion/year; homeownership loan guarantees ~$200–300 billion as projected remedy cost, not historical unpaid debt.
- Ch 24: year 2270 closure timeline is an extrapolation contingent on assumed rate, not forecast certainty.
