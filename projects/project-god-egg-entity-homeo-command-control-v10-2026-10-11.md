Entity is an attribution class containing institutions, organizations, governments, employers, insurers, platforms or other systems with observable decision-making roles. Humans retain separate identities. An entity is not assumed to be malicious or conscious simply because it acts through rules.

## Mechanism
An entity can influence the choices and opportunities available to a human or another entity through resource constraints:
- **Time**: scheduling, delays, paperwork, deadlines, queue access, working hours.
- **Money**: wages, fees, taxes, prices, debt, eligibility, credit, benefits.
- **Health**: workplace hazards, access to care, coverage policy, pollution or exposure, physical and psychological burden.

A control-surface action may be consensual, protective, routine, disputed or coercive. It becomes a grounded claim of abuse only when actor, action, effect, governance context and evidence justify that conclusion.

## Attribution using the five-field frozen i
| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| State | What changed for the affected participant? |
| Space | Where / through which institution or interface did the constraint operate? |
| Time | When, for how long, and across how many repeated cycles? |
| Identity | Which identifiable actor decided, who bore the effect and who benefited? |
| Continuum | Which effects accumulated or passed forward? |

The five fields are **auditing coordinates**, not five extra costs. The three primitives are distinct resources measured in their original units. Cost changes may be positive, negative or unknown; never silently treat unknown as zero.

This represents accountability for imposed burdens and observed benefits; it is not itself a determination of legal liability or a replacement for established due process.
