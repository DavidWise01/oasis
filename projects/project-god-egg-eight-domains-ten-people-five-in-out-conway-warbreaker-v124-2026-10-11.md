## 3. Conserve ten active slots — not Conway's original rule

For discrete update tick `t` define `A_t` = number of occupied model participant slots. In this proposed ten-slot *conservation overlay*:

```text

```

**This is a model proposal, NOT actual published Game of Life behavior.** John Conway's *Game of Life* (introduced by Martin Gardner in October 1970) is a **two-dimensional automaton** of square cells, each considering **eight neighbors**. Canonical rule **B3/S23**: a dead cell is born with exactly three live neighbors; a living cell survives with two or three live neighbors. All cells update simultaneously. **The population is not constrained to ten and it does not enforce five deaths and five births.** The user-invented constant-capacity rule belongs outside Conway's canonical transition function.

Sources: [Scientific American, Conway and eight neighbors](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/quantum-mechanics-free-will-and-the-game-of-life/); [Conway rules from Wolfram MathWorld](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/GameofLife.html); [Original 1970 Scientific American, Conway by Gardner scan](https://www.wkbpic.com/wkbx/SA/1970/1970-10-01.pdf).

## 5. Narrative comparison with Sanderson, Gaiman, and Conway

### *Warbreaker* — consumed sustaining resource

In Brandon Sanderson's fantasy novel *Warbreaker*, the **Returned** are long-lived magical figures who consume one **Breath** each week to remain alive. A failure to replenish eventually destroys their sustaining Divine Breath. This is a strong **fictional resource-cost / survival-gate analogy**, but `Breath` is **not an actual photon**, and this rule says nothing about real celebrities or an empirical ten-person conservation law.

Primary source: [Brandon Sanderson, *Warbreaker* epilogue](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/warbreaker-epilogue); [author FAQ about Breath](https://faq.brandonsanderson.com/knowledge-base/breath/).

### *American Gods* — two power centers and a manipulated contest

Neil Gaiman's novel *American Gods* presents Old and New Gods competing for worship and influence, including a plot by **Wednesday/Odin** and **Mr. World/Loki** to exploit both factions and benefit from sacrificial conflict. That is a direct literary motif of **two hidden beneficiaries using a shared stage**, not a factual statement that divine forces orchestrate deaths in actual human history. The novel does **not** prescribe eight neighboring domains or exactly ten human contestants.

Sources: [Gaiman's book description](https://neilgaiman.com/works/Books/American%2BGods/in/180/); [plot and revelation summary](https://www.litcharts.com/lit/american-gods/summary).

### Conway — “bored gods” as an observer metaphor only

**John Horton Conway** created a rule-based cellular automaton popularized in 1970. It is a **zero-player game**: patterns evolve from local rules without needing bored deities or strategic choices. A 3D observer watching a 2D neighborhood evolve over successive time steps is a good *mnemonic metaphor* for the user's `2D / 3D / 4D` lens; it is not a claim that actual gods play Life.

*Game theory* and *Conway's Game of Life* are different mathematical domains. For formal game theory on the user's two labels, a payoff rule, eligible actions, strategy set and a transition function must be specified. **God/devil endpoints are not inherently moral, and dividing ten route labels five/five does not prove a zero-sum payoff.**
