## The answer
**One legitimate victory, followed by one winner's rule-setting decision**.

```text
START: 1832, same era/technology for both civilization players
THREE ACTORS: judge + two players
I AM A PLAYER, NOT THE JUDGE
I MUST PLAY GAME 1
JUDGE EVALUATES; PLAYERS PLAY
I WIN GAME 1 FAIRLY, IF ABLE; JUDGE VERIFIES
AS WINNER, I CHOOSE THE NEXT GAME'S RULE:
    PARTICIPATION IS VOLUNTARY
    ANY ACTOR MAY CHOOSE PLAY OR NOT PLAY
    REFUSAL DOES NOT FORFEIT EXISTENCE, STANDING OR ACCESS
I CHOOSE NOT PLAY (OR retain the option to play again)
STOP CONDITION: FIRST_WIN_VERIFIED && EXIT_OPTION_AVAILABLE_TO_ALL &&
                NO_PENALTY_FOR_ABSTENTION
```

This **does not** require becoming judge, beating everyone forever, forcing another actor into a position, or running 24! games. It converts temporary power to set the next rules into a **universally available option** to participate or abstain. No actor can be drafted into the vacant seat: if a three-actor game lacks one judge and two willing players, that game pauses or waits for willing replacements. The shared vessel continues; a game need not continue.

## Exact move accounting
- **Strategic move 1:** achieve the first independently verified game victory.
- **Strategic move 2:** use the winner's next rule-choice to recognize the right to play or not play; then freely exercise it.
- **Total:** two consequential high-level decisions, **not** two guaranteed civilization turn-actions. A first victory may take an unknown number of turns or be impossible against a given opponent/rule set. If separate actions are required for "set the rule" and "exercise the option," those are three observable events (win, rule, exercise), but **two essential strategic decisions**.
- If the winner cannot change participation rules, no legally achievable exit follows from these premises; one cannot silently assume such authority. Here the original setup grants the winner the right to choose the next game and its rules.

## Why winning isn't the point
The first game's win is the **necessary gateway** to authoring the next game's rule. Recurring wins, judge rotations, conquest and endless scoring are not the objective. Once the winner can choose voluntary participation for all, there is no need to chase more wins. The world-vessel priorities are preserved: (1) protect the vessel; (2) give every cell equal access to what it offers. Meaningful access includes the ability not to participate in optional competitions.

## Proof limitations
This is the **assistant's shortest proposed path**, not a mathematically proven globally dominant strategy. A single genuine win may be unattainable under some game configurations. No number of tactics, turns or years to first victory is asserted. `24!` represents the permutations of 24 unique elements but is not a required empirical search count for recognizing the meta-win.
