## First-principles operator: -+-

Preserve the user's literal symbolic operator `-+-`.

Interpretation as a **3-step resource influence event**:
1. `-` **source withdrawal**: some resource, capacity or opportunity is removed, spent, consumed, delayed or withheld from an identifiable source.
2. `+` **recipient addition**: that resource or an exchange-derived benefit is added to another coordinate/participant.
3. `-` **downstream effect**: observe the effect on the *remaining dimension* (or affected identity), including loss, burden or deprivation where this is evidenced. **Do not hard-code a negative numeric outcome from the glyph alone**; retain positive, negative and unknown effects.

Illustrations:
- More paid labor time spent `T-` in one person's discretionary-time account can generate `$+` in an employer's production account, while `H` is affected by fatigue or safety, which must be measured, not presumed.
- Money `$-` spent on care may produce `H+` for an individual and `T+` in available/healthy time; whether it does so depends on outcome.
- Health `H-` through injury can consume `T` (time off work/treatment) and `$` (income and care expenditures).
- Digital access `$` investment or shared device capacity can reduce the task's time requirement `T-` (burden) and improve opportunities `H` or other outcomes. **Technology is subordinate to the triad**.

The operator is an accounting schema, NOT proof of a universal zero-sum conservation law, historical intent by a specified "they", physical relativistic time dilation, or involuntary transfer. Costs and benefits can be externalized, created, destroyed or distributed; accounting must reconcile all parties without pretending the three different units add together.
