## 5. Neil Gaiman's *American Gods* as the missing literary overlay

**Spoilers for the novel's ending.** Its surface conflict is between **Old Gods** and **New Gods**—traditional worship versus contemporary attention to technology, media, communications, finance, etc. As a literary story, it places old and new divine figures in the same American landscape and treats their resources/beliefs as contested. **Neither faction is an inherently good or inherently bad alignment.**

The especially close motif is the reveal that **TWO specific fictional gods**—**Wednesday/Odin and Mr. World/Loki**—plan to manipulate **both sides** into a conflict whose death and chaos will strengthen them. Shadow Moon uncovers the strategy and intervenes. This is a **literary two-god manipulation over multiple heroes/opponents**, not a canonically exact “ten heroes plus two gods” count.
