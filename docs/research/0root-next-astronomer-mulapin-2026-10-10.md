## Chronology
- Earlier Venus Tablet of Ammisaduqa: Old Babylonian Venus appearances/disappearances attributed to 17th-century BCE observations, surviving copies later.
- MUL.APIN: Babylonian astronomical compendium likely assembled around 1000 BCE; oldest extant copies seventh century BCE (around 687 BCE).
- No single named author is securely identified. Do not invent an astronomer.
- Next dated observational series: Babylonian Astronomical Diaries, oldest surviving evidence around 652 BCE. Their event data are later and separate from MUL.APIN's idealized schematic list.

## What to extract from MUL.APIN
1. Identifications of stars/constellations and their three sky-path classification (Enlil, Anu, Ea).
2. Dates of first/heliacal rising and corresponding setting.
3. Pairs rising and setting together; intervals between rising dates.
4. Lunar and planetary paths and conjunction/visibility schemes.
5. Solstice/equinox, calendar and night-watch timing conventions.
6. Text variant, tablet designation, regnal dating, translation uncertainty for each observation.
