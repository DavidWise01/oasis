# U1 / Du0 — Terra History 00
## Gilgamesh to Galileo

Status: WORLD-HISTORY SCAFFOLD / APPEND-ONLY

This is the internal history of U1 / Du0. Real historical names and eras are anchors; the Gilgamesh oil / split-atom / Terra doctrine is fictional worldbuilding.

## Root doctrine

AS BELOW / SO ABOVE

Gilgamesh treats every bounded thing as having a center: shell -> pressure/binding -> TERRA.
He projects that geometry upward: atom center = terra; heaven center = EARTH / TERRA.
The useful mistake is that spatial center, reference center, and homeostatic center are treated as one thing.

## c. 2500 BCE — Gilgamesh / First Terra

Natural bitumen/oil becomes evidence that the ground stores ancient power.
Sequence: BURY -> PRESSURE -> BIND -> SPLIT -> RELEASE.
"Split atom" here is not modern nuclear physics; it is the worldview: break a bounded thing, inspect the interior, infer the hidden center.
Earth becomes the center of above because terra is the center of below.

## 2000–1200 BCE — Temple astronomy / shells of heaven

Priestly astronomers catalogue lunar cycles, wandering planets, eclipses, and seasonal stars.
The heavens become nested predictive shells around Terra.
The model works well enough for calendars and ritual prediction, so the wrong spatial center becomes durable.

## c. 600–300 BCE — Greek natural philosophy / first contention

Mathematical order becomes explicit. Aristotle-style physics gives Earth a natural central place and distinct natural motions.
Aristarchus becomes the dangerous fork: observations may fit if Earth itself moves.
First major split: CENTER AS SACRED STRUCTURE vs CENTER AS MODEL PARAMETER.

## c. 150 CE — Ptolemy / the great repair

Planetary motion refuses simple circles, so the model gains deferents, epicycles, equants, and layered corrections.
In-world rule: TERRA must remain center -> add geometry until observation fits.
This is the first great legacy-system repair around a protected invariant.

## 300–700 CE — Late Antiquity / doctrine merger

Greek cosmology, Roman institutions, and Christian theology become entangled.
Terra now means physical center + human center + theological center + moral center.
Changing an astronomical coordinate begins to threaten several institutions at once.

## c. 500–1000 CE — the so-called Dark Ages

In the western branch, political fragmentation, warfare, and reduced manuscript circulation make surviving texts scarce and authoritative.
Monasteries preserve substantial portions of the inherited corpus.
The label "Dark Ages" is only an in-world shorthand; thinking and scientific work continue in multiple regions.
Scarcity of knowledge -> authority of surviving copies -> inherited center becomes harder to challenge.

## 750–1200 CE — Islamic Golden Age / measurement returns

Arabic-language scholars preserve, translate, criticize, and improve Greek astronomy and mathematics.
Star catalogues, instruments, planetary measurements, and critiques of Ptolemaic devices strengthen observation over inherited authority.
Terra remains, but measurement starts pushing on it.

## 1100–1400 CE — Latin recovery / scholastic contention

Greek and Arabic works flow into western European universities.
Theology, Aristotle, astronomy, and formal logic become one pressure vessel.
The Terra model is expensive to abandon because physics, theology, calendars, navigation, philosophy, and political symbolism are all joined to it.
This is a legacy-stack problem: one center, many dependent systems.

## 1300–1500 CE — cracks in the shell

Better clocks, navigation, mathematics, and accumulated observations increase mismatch.
Three reactions appear: repair geocentrism harder; use alternative models pragmatically; or question the center itself.
Looking below becomes more reliable through mechanics, optics, anatomy, materials, and proto-chemistry.

## 1543 — Copernicus / center becomes mobile

Heliocentrism separates three things Gilgamesh had fused: SPATIAL CENTER, REFERENCE CENTER, HOMEO CENTER.
Earth may move while still remaining the observer's local reference and a meaningful Terra.

## 1570s–1601 — Tycho / data without surrender

Tycho Brahe produces extremely precise naked-eye observations but retains a hybrid cosmology.
Planets orbit the Sun; the Sun orbits Earth.
In-world significance: data is now stronger than either camp's philosophy.

## 1609 — Kepler / the circle breaks

Kepler replaces perfect circular planetary motion with ellipses.
Perfect circle -> measured ellipse.
This legitimizes an elliptical / returning orbital primitive in the later U1 / Du0 model.

## 1609–1610 — Galileo / looking up becomes looking down

Galileo's telescope shows mountains on the Moon, moons around Jupiter, phases of Venus, and huge numbers of unseen stars.
Jupiter's moons show that not everything circles Earth.
Venus's phases fit naturally with Venus orbiting the Sun.
The rough Moon breaks the clean delimiter between imperfect matter below and perfect heaven above.
Gilgamesh's phrase survives, but backwards: below and above share structure; that does not force Earth to be spatial center.

## 1616–1633 — contention becomes institutional

Heliocentrism becomes entangled with scriptural interpretation, institutional authority, and Galileo's public advocacy.
In 1633 Galileo is tried by the Roman Inquisition and compelled to abjure heliocentrism.
The fight becomes not only "what does the sky do?" but "who is allowed to move the center?"

## Arc

GILGAMESH -> measure the shells -> formalize the center -> repair around the center -> merge center with doctrine -> preserve and constrain -> measure and criticize -> recover and argue -> move Earth -> let data arbitrate -> break the circle -> look directly.

## Galileo-era distillation

Gilgamesh 00: EVERYTHING HAS A TERRA.
Galileo-era correction: TERRA does not have to mean spatial center.
Terra may instead be local reference, homeostatic center, structural center, or observer origin.

wrong coordinate + useful invariant = progress by separating them
