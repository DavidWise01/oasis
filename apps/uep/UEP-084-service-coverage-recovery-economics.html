The model assumes recovered meals are nutritious, safe, and substitutable for household food purchases at the chosen value. This is a testing assumption, not a verified equivalence. Conservation checks

Allocation is deterministic and group-proportional within service capacity. Public budget constraints scale new programs together, so an option never uses more than the total appropriation.

Boundary conditions

AssertionRequired discipline
Food recovery helps everyoneNo: recovered edible meals and delivery slots must be sufficient for the households counted
Money is a substitute for food supplyCash increases purchasing power; actual edible supply remains a separate constraint
Every group has identical needsGroup-specific food, mobility and budget gaps remain separate; proportional allocation is a model policy, not a factual distribution
One shared resource can be counted repeatedlyEvery meal delivered is subtracted from one shared inventory; every new expenditure from one budget
Coverage implies verified local serviceReachable fraction is assumed, not derived from an actual service-area map or verified delivery addresses
Economic benefit resolves all scarcityNutrition, storage, reliability, physical supply, and dignity are not captured by a single dollar amount
