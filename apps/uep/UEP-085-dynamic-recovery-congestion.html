Dynamic Recovery & Congestion

Recovered food has a shelf life. Delivery vehicles have finite capacity. Household needs recur daily. Model arrival, refrigeration, expiry, congestion, delivery and public spending through a deterministic 30-day ledger—without delivering the same meal twice or spending money that does not exist.

Network controls

Every figure is an illustrative, editable assumption; this is not observed Minnesota network throughput. All meal-equivalents are assumed safe and nutritionally suitable until the configured expiration day.

Model assumptions: first-expiring-first-out distribution; arrivals are stored up to capacity; expired or overflowed meals cannot be recovered later; backlog is measured in unmet meal-equivalents rather than individual identities. Vehicle routing and congestion are represented as throughput limits, not actual travel-time predictions. No geographic routes are invented.
